Lettr supports two-factor authentication (2FA) to add an extra layer of security to your account. When it's on, signing in with a password, Google or GitHub asks for a second step: a time-based code from an authenticator app, or one of your [passkeys](#using-passkeys-for-two-factor-authentication). You can turn on either method or both.

## Setting Up Two-Factor Authentication

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Security Settings">
    Navigate to **Settings** → **Two-Factor Auth** in the sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up the authenticator app">
    On the **Authenticator app** row, click **Set up**. You'll be prompted to confirm your password.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan the QR Code">
    Open your authenticator app (such as Google Authenticator, Authy, or 1Password) and scan the QR code displayed on screen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter Verification Code">
    Enter the 6-digit code from your authenticator app to confirm the setup.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save Recovery Codes">
    After enabling 2FA, you'll receive a set of recovery codes. **Store these in a safe place** — they're the only way to access your account if you lose your authenticator device.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Store your recovery codes securely (e.g., in a password manager). You can show them again later with **View Recovery Codes**. If you lose your authenticator device or passkeys and your recovery codes, you will not be able to access your account.
</Warning>

## Signing In with 2FA

After enabling two-factor authentication, the sign-in process adds one step:

1. Enter your email and password as usual, or sign in with Google or GitHub
2. When prompted, enter the 6-digit code from your authenticator app, or confirm with a passkey if you use passkeys for 2FA
3. Alternatively, use a recovery code if you can't use either

## Recovery Codes

Recovery codes are single-use backup codes that let you sign in when you don't have access to your authenticator app or passkeys. Each code can only be used once. The authenticator app and passkeys share one set of codes.

To regenerate recovery codes:

1. Go to **Settings** → **Two-Factor Auth**
2. Under **2FA Recovery Codes**, click **View Recovery Codes**, then **Regenerate Codes**
3. Store the new codes securely

<Note>
  Regenerating recovery codes invalidates all previous codes. Make sure to update your stored codes.
</Note>

## Disabling 2FA

To disable two-factor authentication:

1. Go to **Settings** → **Two-Factor Auth**
2. Click **Remove** on the **Authenticator app** row, and turn off the **Passkeys** switch if it's on
3. Confirm your password if asked

2FA stays on while either method is on.

## Passkeys

A passkey lets you sign in with Touch ID, Windows Hello, your phone, or a hardware security key instead of typing your password. Passkeys are tied to your device and can't be phished or reused on another site.

### Adding a Passkey

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Passkeys section">
    Go to **Settings** → **Two-Factor Auth** and find the **Passkeys** section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a passkey">
    Click **Add passkey** and give it a name you'll recognize later, such as *MacBook Pro* or *YubiKey*. Lettr first asks you to confirm it's you with your password or an existing passkey. If you sign in with Google or GitHub and don't have a passkey yet, Lettr emails you a code instead.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm on your device">
    Your browser asks you to confirm with your fingerprint, device PIN, phone, or security key. Once confirmed, the passkey appears in the list.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each passkey in the list shows when it was added and when it was last used. Click **Remove** to delete a passkey you no longer use, for example after replacing a laptop. The name is set when you add the passkey; to change it, remove the passkey and add it again.

### Signing In with a Passkey

On the login page, click **Sign in with passkey** and confirm on your device. A passkey proves both who you are and that you have your device, so it counts as both sign-in steps and you won't be asked for an authenticator code afterwards.

Passkeys also work in these places:

- **Confirm your identity**: when Lettr asks you to confirm your password before a sensitive action, click **Confirm with passkey** instead.
- **Two-factor challenge**: if you sign in with your password and also use an authenticator app, the code screen offers **Use a passkey instead**.
- **Team invitations**: signing in with a passkey from an invitation link accepts the invitation, just like signing in with a password.

### Using Passkeys for Two-Factor Authentication

Adding a passkey doesn't turn on two-factor authentication by itself. To make a passkey your second sign-in step, turn on the **Passkeys** switch under **Settings** → **Two-Factor Auth**. You don't need an authenticator app for this. The switch is available once you've added at least one passkey.

- Turning the switch on asks for your password or an existing passkey, and signs you out on your other devices.
- Signing in with a password, Google or GitHub then asks for one of your passkeys. If you also use an authenticator app, either one works.
- If you don't have recovery codes yet, turning the switch on creates them. Both methods share the same codes.
- Removing your last passkey turns the switch off. If your team requires 2FA and passkeys are your only method, Lettr won't let you remove your last passkey.

<Note>
  Only the **Passkeys** switch counts toward a team's **Require Two-Factor Authentication** setting. A passkey you only use on the login page doesn't. To meet the requirement, turn on the switch, set up an authenticator app, or both.
</Note>

## Requiring 2FA for Your Team

Team owners can require all team members to enable two-factor authentication. When this is enabled, members without 2FA will be redirected to set it up before they can access any team resources.

To enable this requirement:

1. Go to **Settings** → **Team**
2. In the **Security** section, toggle on **Require Two-Factor Authentication**

Members who haven't set up 2FA will see a setup prompt when they try to access the dashboard. They can still sign in, but they won't be able to use any team features until 2FA is configured. Members can meet the requirement with an authenticator app or by turning on the [Passkeys switch](#using-passkeys-for-two-factor-authentication). A passkey without the switch doesn't count.

<Tip>
  The team settings page shows which members have 2FA enabled (green shield icon) and which don't (gray shield with alert). Review this before enabling the requirement to give your team advance notice.
</Tip>

For more about team security settings, see [Teams](/learn/settings/teams).

## Social Login (OAuth)

Lettr supports signing in with **Google** and **GitHub**. Social login provides a convenient alternative to email and password authentication.

When you sign in with a social provider for the first time, your social account is linked to your Lettr account automatically (matched by email address). If you don't have a Lettr account yet, one is created for you with your email automatically verified.

Social login does **not** bypass two-factor authentication. If you have 2FA enabled, you'll still need to complete the 2FA challenge, with your authenticator app, a passkey or a recovery code, after authenticating with your social provider.

For full details, see [Social Login (OAuth)](/learn/settings/social-login).

## Password Management

You can update your password from **Settings** → **Password**. Enter your current password and your new password to make the change.

After changing your password, consider signing out of any other active sessions manually from **Settings** → **Browser Sessions** if you suspect unauthorized access.

## Email Address Changes

Changing your account email address requires your current password for verification. This protects against unauthorized email changes if someone gains temporary access to your session.

To update your email:

1. Go to **Settings** → **Profile**
2. Enter your new email address
3. Confirm your current password
4. Click **Save**
