Transactional and marketing email differ in why they are sent, and email law, mailbox providers and recipients all treat them differently. This page defines both types, covers the gray areas between them and shows how to keep them on separate sending streams in Lettr.

---

## Definitions

### Transactional Email

Transactional emails are a direct response to something the recipient did or something that happened to their account, such as a password reset link, an order confirmation or a shipping update. An email is transactional when it meets both of these criteria:

1. **Triggered by a user action or system event**: the recipient did something (placed an order, requested a password reset, logged in from a new device) or something happened to their account (payment failed, subscription renewed).
2. **Contains information the recipient expects or needs**: the content relates directly to that action or event and is not promotional.

### Marketing Email

Marketing emails are sent at the sender's initiative to promote products, share news, or drive engagement. Newsletters, promotional offers, product announcements, and re-engagement campaigns all fall into this category. The sender decides when and what to send, not the recipient.

---

## Why the Distinction Matters

The distinction has practical consequences in three areas:

- **Legal requirements**: most email regulations treat transactional and marketing email differently. Marketing email typically requires explicit consent and an unsubscribe mechanism. Transactional email has more lenient requirements because the recipient expects it.
- **User expectations**: a user waiting for a password reset link has to get it in seconds; a weekly newsletter can wait.
- **Deliverability**: mailbox providers evaluate sender reputation based on engagement signals. Marketing email naturally has lower open rates and higher complaint rates than transactional email, so when both types share the same sending infrastructure, the weaker marketing metrics count against transactional delivery too.

---

## Comparison Table

| | Transactional | Marketing |
|-|-|-|
| **Trigger** | User action or system event | Sender-initiated schedule or campaign |
| **Consent requirement** | Implied by the user's action (e.g., creating an account) | Explicit opt-in required in most jurisdictions |
| **Unsubscribe requirement** | Generally not required | Required by law (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, etc.) |
| **Timing** | Immediate or near-immediate | Scheduled or batched |
| **Examples** | Password resets, order confirmations, 2FA codes | Newsletters, promotions, product announcements |
| **Engagement** | Opened far more often, because the recipient is waiting for it | Opened less often |
| **Legal basis** | Legitimate interest / contractual necessity | Consent |

## Common Examples of Each Type

### Transactional

| Category | Examples |
|-|-|
| **Authentication** | Password reset links, two-factor authentication codes, magic login links |
| **Account activity** | Welcome confirmation, email verification, account settings changes, login from new device alerts |
| **Commerce** | Order confirmations, payment receipts, shipping notifications, refund confirmations |
| **Billing** | Invoice emails, payment failure notices, subscription renewal confirmations, plan change confirmations |
| **Notifications** | Comment replies, mentions, shared document alerts, collaboration invitations |
| **System alerts** | Usage limit warnings, security alerts, scheduled maintenance notices |

### Marketing

- Weekly or monthly newsletters
- Promotional offers and discounts
- Product announcements and feature updates
- Re-engagement campaigns for inactive users
- Event invitations and webinar reminders
- Seasonal or holiday campaigns

## Gray Areas

Some emails don't fit neatly into either category.

<Tip>
  **When in doubt, treat it as marketing.** If an email contains any promotional content beyond what the user explicitly requested, apply marketing rules: include an unsubscribe link and make sure you have proper consent.
</Tip>

Common gray areas include:

- **Onboarding sequences**: a welcome email triggered by sign-up is transactional, while a multi-step onboarding drip campaign is closer to marketing. If the email is educating the user about the product they signed up for, it leans transactional. If it is upselling or promoting features they did not request, it leans marketing.
- **Review requests**: asking for a product review after a purchase is related to a transaction, but the request itself is promotional. Most regulators consider these marketing emails.
- **Cross-sell content in transactional emails**: adding a "You might also like..." section to an order confirmation blurs the line. CAN-SPAM allows some promotional content in transactional emails as long as the primary purpose remains transactional. GDPR is stricter. Keep promotional additions minimal and clearly secondary.

## Why Separate Your Sending Streams

Transactional and marketing email sent through the same infrastructure share one reputation and one delivery path. Separating them helps in each of these areas.

### Reputation Isolation

Each sending domain builds its own reputation. Spam complaints about a marketing campaign count against the marketing domain, while password resets and order confirmations keep delivering from the transactional one.

### Different Deliverability Profiles

Transactional emails should arrive instantly, because a password reset link or a verification code is only useful while the user is still on the page waiting for it. Marketing emails are typically batched and can tolerate slight delays. Separate streams let transactional email go out immediately through the API, without queuing or batching behind marketing volume.

A transactional email in the spam folder is a product failure: a user who cannot receive a password reset is locked out entirely, and a missing shipping notice leaves a customer guessing about their order.

### Different Compliance Rules

Transactional and marketing emails have different legal requirements. Separating them at the infrastructure level makes compliance easier to manage and audit, because unsubscribe logic, consent checks and suppression lists can be applied to each stream independently.

## How to Separate in Lettr

Lettr offers several ways to separate the two streams: sending subdomains, a per-send flag, template projects, tags and metadata.

### Use Different Subdomains

Configure separate sending subdomains for each email type. This is the most important step for reputation isolation.

| Email Type | Subdomain Example |
|-|-|
| Transactional | `mail.yourdomain.com` |
| Marketing | `campaigns.yourdomain.com` |

Each subdomain builds its own sender reputation independently. See [Sending Domains](/learn/domains/sending-domains) for setup instructions.

### Mark the Stream When You Send

The send API has an `options.transactional` flag, which defaults to `true`. Leave it at the default for password resets, receipts and other triggered email. Set it to `false` for marketing-style sends. Lettr adds `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers only to campaigns. For API sends with `transactional: false`, Lettr does not add them itself, so check the headers of a delivered message and add your own unsubscribe mechanism where the law requires one.

A password reset leaves the flag at its default:

```javascript
const response = await fetch('https://app.lettr.com/api/emails', {
  method: 'POST',
  headers: {
    'Authorization': 'Bearer lttr_your_api_key',
    'Content-Type': 'application/json',
  },
  body: JSON.stringify({
    from: 'no-reply@mail.yourdomain.com',
    to: ['user@example.com'],
    subject: 'Reset your password',
    template_slug: 'password-reset',
    substitution_data: {
      reset_link: 'https://yourdomain.com/reset?token=abc123',
      expiry_minutes: '30',
    },
  }),
});
```

Applications that already send through SMTP (common with frameworks like Laravel, Rails or Django) can use `smtp.lettr.com` on port 587 with STARTTLS, username `lettr` and your API key as the password. Define your content once with [templates](/learn/templates/introduction) so non-developers can update designs without a code deploy.

### Use Different Templates and Projects

Organize your templates into separate [projects](/learn/templates/projects), one for transactional templates and one for marketing templates. Each type can then follow its own design standards and review process.

### Use `tag` for Marketing Emails

When sending marketing emails through the API, include a `tag` to group related sends and track campaign-level metrics in the [Analytics dashboard](/learn/analytics/filtering-and-breakdowns):

```json
POST /api/emails
Authorization: Bearer lttr_xxxxxxxxxxxx

{
  "from": "news@campaigns.yourdomain.com",
  "to": ["recipient@example.com"],
  "template_slug": "summer-sale",
  "tag": "summer-sale-2025",
  "substitution_data": {
    "first_name": "Alex",
    "offer_code": "SAVE20"
  }
}
```

Transactional emails typically do not need a `tag` since they are triggered individually. See [Tags](/learn/sending/tags) for details.

### Use Metadata for Internal Tracking

Metadata on each API call classifies emails for filtering in your internal systems:

```json
{
  "from": "no-reply@mail.yourdomain.com",
  "to": ["user@example.com"],
  "template_slug": "password-reset",
  "metadata": {
    "email_type": "transactional",
    "triggered_by": "password_reset_request"
  }
}
```

[Webhook](/learn/webhooks/introduction) events carry this metadata, so your handler can route on it.

## Compliance Differences

The two most common frameworks treat transactional and marketing email differently:

| Requirement | CAN-SPAM (Transactional) | CAN-SPAM (Marketing) | GDPR (Transactional) | GDPR (Marketing) |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| **Consent** | Not required | Opt-out model (can send until unsubscribed) | Legitimate interest or contractual basis | Explicit opt-in required |
| **Unsubscribe link** | Not required | Required, opt-outs honored within 10 business days | Not required | Required, as easy to withdraw as to give, honored promptly |
| **Physical address** | Not required if purely transactional | Required | Not a GDPR requirement | Not a GDPR requirement |
| **Accurate sender info** | Required | Required | Required | Required |
| **Honest subject lines** | Required | Required | Required | Required |

<Warning>
  This table is a simplified overview, not legal advice. Email regulations vary by jurisdiction and are subject to change. Consult legal counsel for your specific situation.
</Warning>

## Best Practices

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do not mix promotional content into transactional emails">
    Keep coupon codes and product recommendations out of order confirmations, or keep them minimal. If promotional content becomes the primary purpose of the email, regulators and mailbox providers treat it as marketing, and the transactional sending reputation suffers with it. Even a "You might also like..." block can cause stricter filtering and, under GDPR, legal complications.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Use separate subdomains for each email type">
    This is the single most effective way to protect transactional deliverability, because complaints on the marketing subdomain do not affect password reset delivery on the transactional subdomain. Configure each subdomain with its own DNS authentication records in Lettr.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Always include an unsubscribe mechanism in marketing emails">
    Every marketing email must have a clear, functional unsubscribe link. Lettr adds `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` (one-click) headers to campaigns automatically. For API sends with `transactional: false` it does not add them itself, so check a delivered message's headers. Making it easy to unsubscribe reduces spam complaints, which protects your reputation far more than retaining a disengaged subscriber.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Keep transactional emails focused and fast">
    Transactional emails should contain exactly what the user needs. A password reset email needs the reset link and minimal context, and every extra element adds load time and rendering complexity. Deliver them immediately via the API without batching.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Monitor each stream independently">
    Track deliverability metrics (bounce rate, complaint rate, open rate) separately for transactional and marketing email. A healthy transactional stream and a struggling marketing stream require very different responses. Use Lettr's webhook events and campaign tracking to maintain visibility into each stream.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Handle failures in your application">
    Use webhook handlers to respond to delivery failures. When a password reset email bounces, the application should offer an alternative recovery flow, and when a 2FA code is delayed, the user should be able to request a new one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Set up authentication and a plain text version">
    Transactional email without SPF, DKIM and DMARC is likely to be filtered or rejected by major mailbox providers. Include a plain text alternative alongside the HTML version, since spam filters view HTML-only email with more suspicion.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
