Your domain verified without trouble, and then one day it shows a red **Not working** badge, a DNS warning on a record, or an alert in your inbox. Nothing changed in Lettr, so something changed in your DNS. This guide explains what Lettr checks after verification, what the warnings mean, and how to get the domain healthy again.

If your domain has never verified, start with [Domain Verification Failures](/knowledge-base/troubleshooting/domain-verification) instead.

---

## Why a Verified Domain Breaks

DNS records are not permanent. A record that passed verification months ago can stop working without anyone touching Lettr:

- A teammate deletes or "cleans up" records at your DNS provider.
- You move DNS hosting (new registrar, new nameservers) and forget to copy the Lettr records.
- A tracking or storage CNAME is repointed at another server.
- A domain registration expires or lapses.
- A DNS-level change such as turning on a proxy for a CNAME rewrites the answer Lettr sees.

Lettr has two separate mechanisms that notice this. They behave differently, so it helps to know which one you are looking at.

| Mechanism | When it runs | What it does |
|-|-|-|
| Daily DNS health check | Every day at 03:00 | Looks at your verified domains and raises alerts. Never changes a domain's status. |
| Verification (**Verify DNS Records**, the API, or the MCP tool) | When you or your integration trigger it | Re-checks each DNS record and updates its stored status. |

---

## The Daily Health Check (03:00)

Every day at 03:00, Lettr runs a health check across the verified domains on your team: sending, tracking, inbound, and storage.

The check is **read-only**. It does one DNS lookup per domain and records whether it passed. It never writes a verification status back, never disables a domain, and never stops your sending. It only observes and warns.

What each domain type is checked for:

| Domain type | What the check looks at |
|-|-|
| Tracking | The CNAME still points at Lettr's tracking origin |
| Storage | The CNAME still points at Lettr's storage origin |
| Sending | The domain still has DNS records at all |
| Inbound | The domain still has DNS records at all |

<Note>
  For sending and inbound domains the daily check confirms that the domain still exists in DNS. It does not re-validate SPF, DKIM, or DMARC values. Those are re-checked when verification runs.
</Note>

Before judging your domain, the check confirms Lettr's own DNS resolution is working by looking up a control domain. If that lookup fails, the run is skipped, so a problem on Lettr's side does not produce false alarms for your team.

The result is stored on every run, healthy or not. When you fix the DNS record, the next 03:00 run clears the warning on its own, as long as **Enable alerts** and **Domain DNS Health** alerts are both on.

<Warning>
  The daily check, and with it the Not working badge, only updates while alerts are enabled (**Settings → Alerts → Enable alerts**) and **Domain DNS Health** alerts are on. With either turned off, the check does not record new results, so an existing badge stays as it was.
</Warning>

## What You See When a Check Fails

When a domain fails the daily check, Lettr tells you in several places:

- **Not working badge** — a red badge next to the domain in the domain lists. It shows what the last daily check saw, not the stored verification status of the records.
- **Warning panel** — on the domain's detail page, explaining the consequence in plain terms and asking you to fix the DNS record and re-verify.
- **Dashboard alert** — a critical alert titled, for example, "Sending domain not working".
- **Notification center** — a notification titled "DNS problem on yourdomain.com" with a **Check domain** link. This one always arrives, even if you have turned off the email channel.
- **Email** — if the email channel is enabled, the team owner gets one email per daily run listing the domains whose alerts are due that day. A record that is still broken but already inside its backoff (see below) is left out of the email.
- **Webhook** — if you configured a webhook URL, a `domain_dns_regression` event is delivered with the domain, its type, and the failing records.

The warning panel states what the failure means for that domain type:

| Domain type | What Lettr tells you is affected |
|-|-|
| Sending | Emails you send from this domain may bounce or land in spam |
| Tracking | Open and click tracking is broken, and links in campaigns you already sent may not work |
| Storage | Images in emails you have already sent are not loading |
| Inbound | Email sent to this domain is not being delivered |

Alert settings, including the cooldown and the webhook, live under **Settings → Alerts**. See [Monitoring & Alerts](/learn/settings/alerts#domain-dns-health-alerts) for the full setup.

## Why You Don't Get the Same Alert Every Day

A record that stays broken does not page you forever. Alerts for a still-failing record back off:

- On the default 24-hour cooldown, you are alerted on day 0, 1, 4, and 11.
- After that the alerts stop, but the domain keeps showing **Not working** until the daily check next finds the record fixed.
- Backoff is tracked per record per domain. A newly broken record alerts immediately, even while another record is still failing.
- When a record recovers, its schedule resets. If it breaks again later, alerts start from day 0.

<Tip>
  Silence from Lettr does not mean the problem went away. If you stopped receiving emails about a domain but the badge is still there, the DNS record is still broken.
</Tip>

You can change the base cooldown (1 to 720 hours) or turn DNS health alerts off under **Settings → Alerts → Domain DNS Health**. Turning them off also stops the badge from updating.

## The Grace Period and the Three-Failure Rule

The daily check only warns. The stored status of a record changes through verification, and verification is deliberately forgiving so that one flaky DNS lookup does not knock out a working domain. This applies to sending, tracking and storage domains; inbound MX verification has no grace period.

When a record that was `valid` fails a verification run, Lettr does not mark it `invalid` straight away:

1. **Within 10 minutes of the last successful verification**, a failure is ignored. The record stays `valid` and no failure is counted.
2. **Once those 10 minutes have passed**, each failing run is counted. The first and second failures keep the record `valid` but show a warning on the record card.
3. **On the third consecutive failure**, the record flips to its failing status (for example `invalid` or `missing`).
4. **A single passing run resets the counter** to zero.

The warning wording on the record card:

| Failures | Message shown |
|-|-|
| 1 | A DNS check returned an unexpected result. Your record is still active — this is likely a temporary issue. |
| 2 | Multiple DNS checks have returned unexpected results. Your record is still active — please verify your DNS configuration if this persists. |

<Note>
  Failures are counted per verification run, not per day. Clicking **Verify DNS Records** three times in a row against a broken record will flip it, as will three API or MCP verification calls. The 03:00 health check does not count toward the three.
</Note>

A record that was never valid is not given any grace. It simply shows the result of the latest check.

## What Happens to Sending

How much a broken record affects you depends on what is broken and what the stored status says.

- **While only the daily check is failing**, nothing in Lettr is switched off. The check is informational, so your domain keeps its stored status. The real-world risk is on the receiving side: with a missing or changed record, mailbox providers can no longer authenticate your mail, so messages may bounce or land in spam.
- **After a record flips to invalid** through verification, the domain no longer counts as fully verified, and sends from it are refused:
  - **API and scheduled sends** — Lettr only accepts a send when the domain is **Approved**, DKIM and DMARC are `valid`, and the routing record (CNAME or SPF) is `valid`. Otherwise the request fails with HTTP `400`, `error_code` `unconfigured_domain`, and the message *The sender domain is not configured or approved for sending.* This applies to both the send and schedule endpoints.
  - **Campaigns** — the same conditions apply, and the campaign sender check fails with *The domain "yourdomain.com" is not verified for sending.*

<Warning>
  Do not wait for the third failure. Real receivers evaluate your DNS on every message, so a broken DKIM or SPF record can hurt delivery before Lettr changes any status.
</Warning>

## How to Fix It

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the domain">
    Click **Check domain** in the notification or email, or go to **Domains** and open the domain marked **Not working**. The warning panel lists the failing record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare against Lettr's records">
    On the domain page, compare the records Lettr expects with what your DNS provider actually has. For tracking and storage domains the CNAME must point at the exact target shown.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check what the world sees">
    Use `dig` to confirm the record is publicly visible. The commands are in [Checking DNS Records Yourself](/knowledge-base/troubleshooting/domain-verification#checking-dns-records-yourself).

    ```bash
    dig TXT scph0724._domainkey.yourdomain.com +short
    dig CNAME links.yourdomain.com +short
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Restore or correct the record">
    Re-create the missing record or fix the value at your DNS provider. If you moved DNS hosting, copy every Lettr record to the new provider. If your provider proxies CNAMEs, set them to DNS only.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Re-verify">
    Back on the domain page, click **Verify DNS Records**. This refreshes each record's stored status and resets the failure counter. You can also call the [verification endpoint](/knowledge-base/troubleshooting/domain-verification#verifying-via-api).
  </Step>
</Steps>

Two different things can need clearing, and they clear differently:

- **Health warning only** (the Not working badge, warning panel, and dashboard alert): these are written only by the daily check, so they clear at the first 03:00 run after the record resolves again, provided **Enable alerts** and **Domain DNS Health** are both still on in **Settings → Alerts**. Re-verifying does not clear them, and you do not need to.
- **Stored status flipped to invalid** (the record card shows a failing status and sends are refused): this changes only through verification, so it returns to `valid` only when you click **Verify DNS Records**. Do this as soon as the DNS is fixed rather than waiting for the daily check, because the badge can disappear overnight while the stored status is still invalid.

<Tip>
  Not sure which record is wrong? Ask [Adamko](/learn/ai-assistant/troubleshooting-and-diagnostics#domain-verification-problems), the AI assistant. It runs live DNS lookups, compares each record against what Lettr expects, and points at the one that differs.
</Tip>

## Preventing Drift

- **Keep a copy of your Lettr records** outside your DNS provider, so you can restore them after a migration. The domain page has a **Send to developer** menu that can export them as CSV.
- **Review DNS before cleanups.** Records at `_domainkey`, `_dmarc`, and your tracking, storage, and bounce subdomains look unused but are not.
- **Plan DNS migrations.** Add all Lettr records to the new provider before changing nameservers.
- **Make sure alerts reach a person.** Alerts go to the team owner. Keep the email channel on, or add a webhook, so a broken record does not sit unnoticed.
- **Renew your domain.** An expired domain stops resolving, and the daily check will flag it.

## When to Contact Support

Contact **support@lettr.com** or use the in-app chat if:

- The domain still shows **Not working** more than a day after you fixed the record and the record is visible with `dig`.
- Verification reports a record as `invalid` when it matches the expected value exactly.
- You receive alerts for a domain you already deleted.

Include the domain name and the `dig` output.
